City Infrastructure Briefing

Two systems get bundled
under one name — sewage & flood recovery.

They're engineered separately. One handles water your city makes — rain and waste. The other holds back water trying to get in — tides, surge, sea-level rise. NYC, Boston, and London all run both, but only London ever had to solve just one.

01 — The Vertical Stack

What moves through the sewage system, in order

Three stages, engineered to work as one continuous path from street to river. Capacity gets tested at every joint.

STAGE 01

Collection network

Catch basins and storm drains at street level feed underground pipes. Most people never think about this layer — but it's where capacity gets exceeded first.

If a basin clogs or the pipe beneath is undersized for today's rainfall, water backs up before it ever reaches a plant.
STAGE 02

Storage & treatment

The part cities are racing to build now. A CSO storage tunnel or tank acts as a shock absorber during storms.

Gowanus's tank holds 4 million gallons. London's Tideway tunnel does the same job at 25km — the entire city's excess flow.
STAGE 03

Discharge

Treated water is released to the river or harbor once the storm passes and capacity frees up.

The goal: overflow becomes a rare event, not a weekly one.
02 — The Separate Box

Coastal defense solves a different problem entirely

This box is architecturally unrelated to the sewer system. It's not about water your city generates — rain, waste. It's about water trying to come in from outside: tides, storm surge, sea-level rise.

Floodwalls, deployable gates, and elevated infrastructure belong here. Sewage upgrades solve a completely different problem than a floodwall does — which is why cities need both budgets running in parallel, rather than one solving the other.

London

Solved box 01 only. One deep tunnel, one combined sewer network, one city — no coastline to defend at the same time.

New York

Has to solve both boxes at once, on a coastline, while retrofitting a much larger and older combined system underneath one of the densest cities on Earth.

That's the actual gap between them — not ambition, not funding alone, but how many systems have to work at the same time.
